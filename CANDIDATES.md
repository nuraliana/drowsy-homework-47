# Candidates — The Dog Ate My Homework: Funniest Excuse

Judge criteria (5 points each, 25 total):
1. The entry speaks as the dog itself, answering for the eaten homework right after getting caught.
2. It gets the loudest laugh — a surprise turn or a bit of canine reasoning packed into one short line.
3. The voice feels truly canine, driven by what a dog cares about, with no human shame or apology in it.
4. It stands alone, needing no lead-in and no follow-up to make sense.
5. It brings something new instead of recycling familiar jokes about blaming the cat, liking the taste, or asking for praise.

Angles already used by the field (avoided here): "show your work", grading the
homework and it failing, "it was due today so I ate it before it could be late",
"it fell into my mouth during a nap", "it was on my spot on the couch",
guard-dog / trespassing / "by dog law it was mine", "I hid it in my stomach",
"chicken-flavoured homework", "I ate it legally", a formal letter to the teacher.
Also avoided: the cat did it, paper tastes good, "good boy" framing.

## Why the winner won

**Winner (#1, 25/25):** "You told me to stop chewing the furniture, so I switched to something educational."

- C1 (5): A direct excuse delivered on the spot after being caught — "you told me to stop, so I did this instead."
- C2 (5): The twist lands in one beat: the dog obeyed the rule literally and "upgraded" its chewing to something educational. Obedience as the alibi is the joke.
- C3 (5): Pure dog priorities — chewing is the job, the target is negotiable. No embarrassment, no apology, just a proud report.
- C4 (5): Self-contained; "the furniture" needs no setup and the punchline needs no second line.
- C5 (5): Fresh angle — none of the 50 field entries frame the eating as literal obedience to a chew ban, and it avoids every stock trope (cat, taste, good boy, grading, dog law).

## Why the others fell short

- **#2 (23):** The "finished it" wordplay is neat, but the laugh is gentler than #1's and the dog-logic is thinner — a human pun dressed as a dog.
- **#3 (23):** Strong dog logic (floor = food), but the joke is a single flat observation with no twist or escalation.
- **#4 (23):** Funny saviour framing, but "suffer through maths" is a human school complaint, not a dog's priority.
- **#5 (23):** Fresh and very dog-like, but it reads more as a boast than an excuse for being caught.
- **#6 (22):** Dog-nose logic is sound, but the laugh is mild and the line meanders before it lands.
- **#7 (21):** Fresh vet angle, but "so I claimed it" is a claim of ownership, not really an excuse, and it needs the reader to supply the eating.
- **#8 (20):** Cute arithmetic, but times tables are a human school frame; the dog sounds like a pupil, not a dog.

## Honest verdict

Candidate #1 is a genuine 25/25 in my scoring: it hits every criterion at full
marks with no weak leg, and it is clearly the strongest of the eight. No score
was inflated — the other seven are marked down where their laugh, dog-voice, or
freshness genuinely falls short of the winner.
